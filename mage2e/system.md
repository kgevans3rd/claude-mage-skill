# Mage: The Ascension, 2nd Edition

A **bring-your-own-rulebook** module. It ships the shape of the game — how the
mechanics fit together and how to run them — and cites its tables rather than
reproducing them. The values come from your own copy of the book.

Citations are to **printed** page numbers. In the reference scan, PDF page =
printed page + 17.

---

## Resolving actions

Everything is one roll. Assemble a Dice Pool, usually **Attribute + Ability**,
throw that many d10s, and count each die that meets or beats the difficulty.

Three constraints the book is explicit about (p.80):

- Never more than two Traits combine into a pool.
- A Trait with a potential value of 10 stands alone — Willpower never combines.
- A normal human tops out around ten dice.
- **Sphere ratings are never rolled.** Magick is rolled on Arete.

Difficulty 6 is standard unless the Storyteller says otherwise.

### Ones, failures and botches

A "one" cancels a success; remove both dice. If more ones are left than
successes, the roll **botches**. If nothing is left on either side, it is a
plain failure.

This matters more than it looks. Two successes against three ones is a botch,
not a failure — a roll can botch *having rolled successes*. Paradox is driven
off botches, so getting this backwards makes magick far safer than intended.

### Multiple actions

Split the Dice Pool between actions. If the pools the actions draw on differ in
size, the **smaller** pool is used (p.81).

### Resisted actions

Both sides roll. Your opponent's successes cancel yours exactly as ones do, and
you score only the margin by which you exceed them.

## Magick

An Effect is declared, not looked up. The player says what the mage is doing,
which Spheres it draws on, and how it appears; the Storyteller decides whether
that reading of reality is **coincidental** — something a Sleeper could explain
away — or **vulgar**, which reality itself will answer for.

## Combat

Three stages, every turn.

**Stage One — Initiative.** Roll Wits + Alertness, difficulty 4. The winner
declares her action *last*, having heard everyone else's, and performs it
*first*. Declare any dice-pool split now, and declare any magick: only one
magick roll may be made in a turn.

**Stage Two — Attack.** Dexterity + Firearms, Melee or Brawl. A dodge is
Dexterity + Dodge, may be declared at any time out of the character's remaining
pool, and each of its successes cancels one of the attacker's.

**Stage Three — Resolution.** Roll damage (difficulty 6), then the target soaks
with Stamina (difficulty 6).

General complications: changing a declared action costs +1 difficulty; an
immobilised target is −2 to hit; and when damage taken exceeds the target's
Stamina, it is stunned and loses its next turn.

### Aggravated damage

Some damage cannot be soaked with Stamina alone and heals only slowly: vulgar
Life magick that rends a pattern rather than working within it; vulgar Prime
attacks and attacks charged with Prime; direct Entropy attacks on a living
body; spirits summoned to attack; and the natural weaponry of supernatural
creatures (p.171).
